Stop Smart Glasses — Resources

Smart glasses raise questions about privacy, consent, harassment, safeguarding, data protection and the design of AI-enabled technology. This resource library brings together regulatory documents, government sources, legal analysis, investigations and real-world examples behind the campaign.

What to do if you're filmed without your consent

If you're concerned you may have been filmed by someone wearing smart glasses without your knowledge, the resources below can help you understand what's involved and where to find further information. This is general signposting, not legal advice — recording and privacy laws vary by country and by the specific circumstances.

Did someone wearing Meta Glasses film you today? Are you sure?

The Guardian investigates non-consensual recording with Meta Glasses, including people secretly filmed at home, at work and at events, and examines how difficult it can be to know when the glasses are recording.

Meta Glasses privacy breach: legal rights and how to take action

Samuels Solicitors, a UK law firm, outlines the legal claims that may be available under English law — including breach of privacy, misuse of private information and harassment — if you've been filmed without consent, and how to get advice on your specific situation.

samuels-solicitors.co.uk ↗

Are smart glasses legal? Recording laws and privacy rules explained (US law)

Even Realities — a smart-glasses retailer — gives an overview of US recording-consent and biometric-data laws (for example state-by-state one-party/two-party consent rules). This covers US law only and should not be read as a guide to UK law.

Meta is first. It won't be alone

The questions raised by smart glasses extend beyond Meta. Apple, Google, Samsung and other technology companies are developing or planning their own AI-enabled glasses and wearable devices.

The choices governments, companies and consumers make now could therefore help determine the social norms surrounding face-worn cameras in the future.

The right to privacy in UK law
